[note.health]
title: Hit points
text: Every bite, blow and trap costs me health: the red bar in the corner. When it runs out, the expedition is
	over. A new level fills it up.

[note.levels]
title: Experience
text: Every creature I kill and every riddle I answer teaches me something. With enough of it I reach a new level:
	more health and more stamina, both filled up at once, and any poison gone. Every fifth level my skin gets tougher,
	every eighth my arm stronger.

[note.stamina]
title: Stamina
text: Jumping and running (Shift) tire me out: the yellow bar. Each jump uses up some of it, and running drains it
	all the time. When I walk or stand, it comes back by itself.

[note.keys]
title: Keys and gates
text: A key opens every gate of its colour on this level. Some gates answer to a lever of their colour instead. The
	gate of a boss's lair stays shut until the boss is dead.

[note.poison]
title: Poison
text: Some stings and bites poison me: the health bar turns green and keeps draining after the fight, armour or not,
	and it can kill me. The same poison again only starts over; a stronger or weaker one runs alongside it. A new level
	clears all poison.

[note.weapons]
title: Weapons
text: Copies of my weapons are in chests, and now and then a creature leaves one; with enough copies a weapon can be
	upgraded in the inventory (U). The old club gains the most from it. 1 takes up the next hand weapon, 2 the next one
	to shoot or throw. Every creature takes each kind of blow differently: I note it down.
	A bow, a sling or a throw has only a few shots ready, the gold marks on its tile; they come back one by one once I
	stop shooting with it for a while. It hits softer than a hand weapon and needs room: a creature too close, and
	there is no shot. I open with it and finish up close.

[note.blunt]
title: Smash
text: A club smashes, and so do the mace, the sling's stones and the throwing stick. They hit hard, but they do not
	cut.

[note.slash]
title: Cut
text: A blade cuts: the bronze sword, the khopesh (a curved sword), the epsilon axe. Long cuts, quick to follow one
	another.

[note.pierce]
title: Stab
text: A point stabs: the flint knife, the spear, the javelin, the duckbill axe, every arrow. It finds the gaps
	between scales and plates.

[note.health-potions]
title: Health potions
text: Bitter herbs from the marshes heal my wounds, the priests' brew twice as much. When I am in a hurry, H drinks
	the one that fits best.

[note.stamina-potions]
title: Stamina potions
text: Date wine, or the red beer of Hathor: a stamina potion gives me my stamina back. When I am in a hurry, 0
	drinks the one that fits best.

[note.might]
title: Blood of Montu
text: It tingles. Whatever is in it, my arm hits harder after I drink it, forever.

[note.armor]
title: Granite of Ptah
text: Ptah's granite: my skin becomes hard as a temple wall, and every blow hurts a little less, forever.

[note.life]
title: Breath of Amun
text: The breath of Amun: more health forever, and all my wounds close at once.

[note.antidote]
title: Milk of Renenutet
text: The milk of Renenutet cures every poison in me. = drinks one, but only while I am poisoned.

[note.dart-traps]
title: Dart traps
text: A slab with a cobra on it sank under my foot, and darts shot out of three holes in the wall above it, tipped
	with venom. A rat is too light to press it, a mummy is not, and the darts hit whoever is in their way. A jump over
	the slab sets nothing off; a jump at the right moment lets them pass under me.

[note.resistance]
title: Resistance potions
text: Cobra venom, a drop at a time. For two minutes a poisoned bite or sting often does nothing to me: half the
	time after the lesser potion, nearly always after the greater one. The greater potion also cures a poison already
	in me. A poison guard amulet adds its share.

[note.heka]
title: Heka
text: The guardian's heka, the magic the gods hold, went into me. It tastes of river mud and old incense. Each
	guardian gives one; on the Heka page (K) I spend it on a skill, forever. There are more skills than guardians down
	here, so I choose with care.

[note.serkets-favour]
title: Serket's favour
text: Serket likes me. Her children still bite, but they hurt me less: every poison on me lasts half as long, with
	the same damage each second. At her shrine she listens twice as long: four minutes without venom. The day she
	took me in, she drew out the venom I carried.

[note.sharp-eye]
title: Sharp eye
text: Horus lent me an eye. I will give it back. Probably. Meanwhile my bows, the sling, the javelin and the
	throwing stick all reach a third farther: even a foe far away is in my sights.

[note.curse-weakness]
title: Weakness
text: A curse sits on my arm: half my might is gone, and the greater curse takes all of it and more. Nothing I drink
	removes a curse. Two minutes, then it fades. Another curse of the same kind restarts the two minutes.

[note.curse-clumsiness]
title: Clumsiness
text: Cursed with clumsy hands: every blow, shot and throw does a fifth less damage, and half as much under the
	greater curse. My venom is not affected. Two minutes and it passes; no potion helps.

[note.curse-vulnerability]
title: Vulnerability
text: Cursed: my armour feels like papyrus. It stops only two fifths of what it did. Two minutes, and nothing cures
	it. I should not get hit in that time.

[note.curse-fatigue]
title: Fatigue
text: A curse drained me: my stamina fell to a fifth and does not come back by itself for two minutes. A stamina
	potion still works.

[note.curse-disease]
title: Disease
text: A cursed sickness: my body holds less health, a third less, much less under the greater curse. It gives the
	health back when it passes, in two minutes. No antidote helps.

[note.curse-slowness]
title: Slowness
text: Cursed legs: I walk and climb at half speed, and I run hardly faster than I used to walk. Two minutes. Traps
	made for a quick step are dangerous for me now.

[note.curse-palsy]
title: Palsy
text: My hands shake from a curse: no bow, sling or throw finds its mark, so I do not try. My melee weapons still
	work. Two minutes.

[note.curse-insanity]
title: Insanity
text: My mind is clouded. The club stays in my hand, and I cannot use my map, journal, pack or potions. Two minutes,
	if I live.

[note.fire]
title: Fire
text: Fire sets what it hits on fire: it keeps burning for a few seconds after the hit. Armour does not stop it,
	only a fire guard amulet does. Water puts it out, and in the water a flame barely scorches. A brazier lights my
	next five arrows (E by it). Mummies, it turns out, burn very well. Whatever shrugs off an arrow, a bat's thin wing,
	a mimic's dry wood, a plant, a mummy, burns instead: fire arrows are my answer to it. Scorpions are used to the
	heat.

[note.favour-of-hapi]
title: Favour of Hapi
text: The water parts for me now. Not much, but enough to stop wading like a heron.

[note.blessing-of-isis]
title: Blessing of Isis
text: Isis has a knot for every curse. She ties fast. The day she blessed me, she untied the curses I carried.

[note.water-blessing]
title: Blessing of Hapi
text: Hapi's jar. For two minutes the Nile and I are on good terms: I wade at full speed, sprint and jump in half
	water as if it were a street. With his favour it lasts twice as long and the water carries me.

[note.canopic-jars]
title: Canopic jars
text: Four jars, four sons of Horus: one each for the liver, the lungs, the stomach and the guts. Somebody swapped
	the insides for treasure. I try not to wonder where the insides went.

[note.jumped-bite]
title: Jumping a bite
text: It snapped at my heels and caught only air. A small one bites low: jump as it opens its jaws and the bite goes
	under me. Over a tall one a jump does not help.

[note.sand]
title: Sand
text: Sand. Underground. Somebody left a door open, three thousand years ago. I wade through the drifts at three
	quarters of my pace, and running in it is out of the question. Jumping still works.

[note.quicksand]
title: Quicksand
text: The sand swallowed my boots, then my shins. Standing still makes it worse: the longer I stay, the slower I
	get, until I am stuck and it squeezes. A jump gets me out, if I have the breath for it. Up close it swirls, slowly.
	Look before you step.

[note.sun-mirrors]
title: Sun mirrors
text: The priests knew how to bring the sun underground. So did a certain archaeologist with a staff. A shaft of
	daylight runs straight until rock stops it; a bronze mirror sends it on the way its pointer shows, and I can turn
	one a quarter at a time. A sun disc, an Aten, that catches the light opens the gates of its colour for good. The
	dead do not like the sun: it sets them alight.

[note.trapped-chests]
title: Trapped chests
text: Some chests bite back. Darts from the lock, a puff of green gas, a curse on the lid, deeper down a nest of
	scarabs. Up close a trapped one looks darker, as if it knew. Half of them were robbed long ago and only the trap is
	left; the rest hold a little more than a plain chest. The Eye of Horus sees most traps before they go off.

[note.tomb-curses]
title: Tomb curses
text: The lid said 'cursed be he who opens this'. I opened it. The lid was right. The dead write their threats on
	whatever they leave behind: lids, jars, the little servant figures. Not every one means it, and the curse runs out,
	as any curse.

# The hour notes (plan campaign-texts): written on entering a zone, one per ZoneId in order.
[note.hour-1]
title: The First Hour
text: The gate of the tomb. Ra's boat enters the Duat and the first gate closes behind it. It closed behind me too,
	and I did not even have a boat. The floors have teeth. Mind the floors.

[note.hour-2]
title: The Second Hour
text: Bats, and a smell of old leather. The wall says the second hour is the water of the blessed, where the grain
	grows. Nothing grows here except my doubts. And the bats.

[note.hour-3]
title: The Third Hour
text: The dark thickens, and the walls are hung with webs. The third hour is the hour of the waters of Osiris. I am
	being judged by spiders anyway. I keep a torch close.

[note.hour-4]
title: The Fourth Hour
text: The priests' quarter. Ra's boat has to be towed over sand here, says the wall, and the priests wrote a great
	deal on it. I can read it at last. I almost wish I could not.

[note.hour-5]
title: The Fifth Hour
text: The cavern of Sokar's secret, the wall says, and in my private chapter: the cavern of Serket's scorpions. I do
	not like her sense of humour. I count my antidotes.

[note.hour-6]
title: The Sixth Hour
text: The midnight of the journey: Ra and Osiris meet in the dark, and the serpents come to watch. Apep is beaten back here. The wall says:
	the dead are made new here. I will settle for staying the same.

[note.hour-7]
title: The Seventh Hour
text: The seventh hour, says the wall: Apep lies on a sandbank, having drunk the river, and the magic of Isis and Seth
	and the spells of Serket bind him. Mehen coils round Ra. The boat is hauled through the cisterns. So am I.

[note.hour-8]
title: The Eighth Hour
text: Sand again, red and endless. Seth stands in the prow of the boat, and the serpent is driven back with spear and spell.
	The wall says the dead here are clothed. I would settle for a hat.

[note.hour-9]
title: The Ninth Hour
text: The ground gives way and the worms sleep. Sokar's land lay hours back, says the wall, but this one is no firmer. The ninth hour feeds the dead with grain,
	the wall says. I would be glad of a sandwich. I would be gladder of a floor.

[note.hour-10]
title: The Tenth Hour
text: A lake of fire, and baboons on its shore. The tenth hour is water and the rescue of the drowned, says the wall, so
	there must be water somewhere. I look for it first. Everything else second.

[note.hour-11]
title: The Eleventh Hour
text: The necropolis. Every trap I have met, in one place, and the road to the last gate. The eleventh hour is the
	one before the dawn. The dawn is not what I would call close.

[note.hour-12]
title: The Twelfth Hour
text: The last hour. Ra passes through the serpent Life of the Gods, ankh-netjeru, tail to mouth, and comes out as
	Khepri at dawn, says the wall. The ankh again, in the serpent's own name. The scales are in my private chapter, and
	I have a feeling that something here will weigh my heart. I did bring it. Let me be light.

text: A jackal's tooth on a cord. Worn, it adds to every blow I strike: Might +1, +2, +4, +6, from the lesser amulet

[note.amulet-armor]
title: Bronze scarab

	the lesser to the grand one.

[note.amulet-health]
title: Carnelian heart
text: A carnelian heart, and it beats with mine: my health is greater by 5, 10, 20 or 30 percent while I wear it,
	from the lesser to the grand one. Taking it off does not hurt me.

[note.amulet-poison]
title: Serket's scorpion
text: Serket's scorpion. Her children spare me: a poisoned bite or sting fails 10, 25, 50 or 80 percent of the time,
	lesser to grand. It adds to a resistance potion's share.

[note.amulet-traps]
title: Eye of Horus
text: The eye of Horus sees the spikes first. Spike, death trap and dart damage is cut by 25, 50, 75 percent, and

[note.amulet-blunt]

[note.amulet-slash]

[note.amulet-pierce]

[note.amulet-regeneration]

text: A faience lotus. It opens when all is calm: while nothing chases me and no poison runs, it heals me, 1 health

[note.amulet-venom]

text: Wadjet's rearing cobra, and my blade grows fangs: 10, 15, 20 or 30 percent of my hits poison the foe, weakly,

[note.amulet-fire]

text: Ra's disc burns daily. Fire does not care about my armour, but this does: fire hurts me 20, 40, 60 or 80
	percent less, lesser to grand. Never nothing at all.

[note.amulet-crushing]

text: The mace head of Seth: what it hits stays hit. Worn, my smashing blows deal 10, 20, 30 or 50 percent more,
	lesser to grand. Only the smashing part counts: a mace gains all of it, a club nearly all.

[note.amulet-cleaving]
title: Montu's khopesh
text: The sickle sword of Montu parts what it meets. Worn, my cutting blows deal 10, 20, 30 or 50 percent more,
	lesser to grand. Only the cutting part counts: a sword gains most of it, a club hardly any.

[note.amulet-piercing]
title: Neith's arrowhead
text: The arrowhead of Neith finds the gap. Worn, my stabbing blows deal 10, 20, 30 or 50 percent more, lesser to
	grand. Only the stabbing part counts: every arrow gains all of it.

[note.amulet-shots]
title: Satet's quiver
text: Satet, the archer of the cataracts, shoots the flood into the land each year. Her little quiver on my chest
	holds more than it should: every bow, sling, throwing stick and javelin I carry holds a quarter, a half, three
	quarters or twice its shots, lesser to grand. My self-bow goes from five to seven, eight, nine, ten.

[note.amulet-quick-shots]
title: Bastet's cat
text: A sitting cat of bronze, Bastet's, and a cat is ready again before the mouse has landed. Worn, my spent
	shots come back a quarter, a half, three quarters or twice as fast, lesser to grand, the wait after the last
	shot too. With the grand cat a bow is ready every second and a quarter.

[note.stun]
title: Stun
text: A good knock on the head and the beast forgets what it was doing: it stands there for a moment and a half,
	stars round its head, looking for the rest of its thoughts. A bat even forgets how to fly. A club or a mace does
	it best (a sling stone too), a sword or a spear never; the gods' great beasts shake it off twice as fast, and not
	twice in a row. Rude, but it works.

[note.rings]
title: The rings
text: A round plate in the floor, rings of bronze hidden in the ceiling over it. I stood on it and pressed: five rings
	came down round me, one after the other, a flash of white, and they rose again in another hall. Another plate,
	the same rings above. It goes both ways. At last, a ring for my Rings pocket. It does not fit on a finger.

[note.stargate]
title: The ring of seven marks
text: A ring of stone taller than two men against the wall, a track of signs inside it, seven marks round its rim. I
	put my hand on it and the track turned: a mark lit with a clunk, then the next, six of them. The seventh flickered
	and went dark. Chevron seven... will not lock. Seven chevrons. Only six symbols on the wall. Daniel would know.

[note.dial-device]
title: The dial
text: A stone lectern before the ring, two circles of signs round a red dome. I pressed the dome. Nothing. I pressed
	it again, harder. Indeed. Whatever it was built to call does not answer, and Abydos is not on this map.

[note.coffins]
title: Coffins
text: Somebody moved out of this coffin long ago and left the furniture. The robbers missed a few things; so did I,
	until I lifted the lid. Read a lid before lifting it. The prayers run down the legs in blue and black; a text in
	red is the other kind, a curse on whoever disturbs the owner. The cursed ones keep a little more inside, which is
	exactly what a curse would say.

[note.sandstorm]
title: Sandstorm
text: Wind, a hundred feet under the desert. It comes through the hall in gusts, on a beat you can count: the hiss
	rises, grit skitters along the floor, then the sand hits and walks me back the way I came. No point fighting it.
	Count, cross in the calm, and wait out the next gust behind a statue. In the gust I can hardly see, or hear what
	is coming. Neither can the rats; the wind takes them too.

[note.shrine-of-isis]
title: The Shrine of Isis
text: A little shrine in the rock, Isis on her throne inside, her wings spread on the wall behind her, bread still on
	the offering table. I knelt and asked her, feeling foolish. Every curse on me lifted at once, and for a while
	none would take: the next one slid off like water. It does not last, two minutes or so, but she does not tire of
	being asked. She hid her son from Seth in the marshes; she can hide one archaeologist for a moment.
	The offering table takes more than bread: a spare amulet or ring, a weapon I have no more use for, even a potion, and I come
	away wiser, the more so the more I already know. Her magic grows an amulet and joins two rings into a finer one; my hands could never do that.

[note.wall-spikes]
title: Wall spikes
text: Spikes in the wall, tipped with venom, and they keep time like a priest at a drum. A scrape just before, then
	they thunk out and stand there; while they are out they are a wall, and whoever is in their cell gets stabbed,
	poisoned, and shoved out. Jumping does nothing, sprinting only shortens the walk. Stand clear, wait for them to
	draw in, cross. Several in a row are out of step on purpose: find the gap they share. A trap ward amulet takes
	the edge off the stab.

[note.fire-jets]
title: Fire jets
text: A vent low in the wall glows and hisses, and then a jet of fire goes up through its cell. It burns hard for as
	long as I stand in it, a jump over it included, and does not stop me from walking in. The beat is the same as the
	spikes': watch the glow, cross in the quiet. A fire guard amulet helps.

[note.poison-drips]
title: Poison drips
text: A green puddle on the floor that poisons whoever wades in (a jump clears it), and over it a lump of venom on
	the ceiling. A creak, and the lump falls: it hits, and poisons worse than the puddle, and a jump does not save me
	from it. Something on the ceiling has been saving that for me. Go round the puddle if there is room, cross the
	cell when the lump is back up.

[note.dodge]
title: Dodge
text: I am quicker than I look. About one blow in ten, a bite, a claw, a dart, a spike, finds only the air where I
	was. No damage, and no poison with it. It is luck, not a shield: never count on it.

[note.power-hit]
title: Power hit
text: Hold the attack and wind up: a melee blow let go after the wind-up hits harder, the longer I held it, but each
	moment costs stamina, and a quarter of my stamina is the most a blow will take. While I hold, nothing regrows and
	I cannot sprint. A tap is a plain swing, free. Q turns it on and off with the other stamina skills.

[note.flaming-arrows]
title: Flaming arrows
text: Sekhmet's flame on the arrowhead. With the stamina skills on (Q), each arrow costs a tenth of my stamina and
	hits two fifths harder, the extra as fire: it sets the target burning and gets past what shrugs off an ordinary
	arrow. With too little stamina the arrow flies plain, and the sign on the screen blinks. A brazier's flame is free
	and needs no skill.

[note.words-of-thoth]
title: Words of Thoth
text: The carved words on the walls were pictures yesterday. Now they are sentences. Thoth keeps his writing for
	those who ask, and every inscription I read pays me in experience, the ones I passed before as well. They are
	mostly warnings and gifts. Mostly.

[note.ka-of-osiris]
title: Ka of Osiris
text: Osiris came back from death; a share of his ka came into me. A quarter more health, and it stays: no amulet
	to lose, no curse to wait out. The new share came full, the old wounds did not close.

[note.strength-of-montu]
title: Strength of Montu
text: Montu, the war god of Thebes, sits in my shoulder now. Five more might in every blow, for good. My club
	hits like a younger man's.

[note.djed-of-ptah]
title: Djed of Ptah
text: Ptah's pillar, the djed, stands in my spine. Five more armor, for good: a bite has to work harder to get
	through.

[note.breath-of-shu]
title: Breath of Shu
text: Shu, the air itself, breathes with me. A quarter more stamina for good: longer sprints, more jumps before I
	am out of breath.

[note.speed-of-mafdet]
title: Speed of Mafdet
text: Mafdet runs down snakes and lends me her feet: a fifth faster walking and sprinting on firm ground. Water
	and sand slow me as before, and a jump carries no farther: the gaps I could not clear, I still cannot.

[note.wisdom-of-seshat]
title: Wisdom of Seshat
text: Seshat measured every temple before it was built, so she knows its hidden rooms. A riddle shows its hint at
	once. A name gate tells me the name it wants. Near a sealed room, its plan draws itself on my map.

[note.disarm-trap]
title: Disarm trap
text: I see the threads now. Four times in five a trapped chest, jar or coffin opens quietly: no darts, no gas, no
	curse, no scarabs. With the Eye of Horus it is safer still. Up close, trapped containers and the mimics that
	pass for them show a red rim. And a trapped one is never empty: where its treasure was taken, something of its
	depth lies instead.

[note.quiver-of-neith]
title: Quiver of Neith
text: Neith counts my arrows. She counts better than I do: two in five more in every bow, sling and javelin
	bundle, and the spent ones come back a quarter sooner. Satet's quiver and Bastet's cat still add their own.

[note.shrine-of-montu]
title: The Shrine of Montu
text: Another little naos, this one painted red. Montu stands in it with a falcon's head and the sun on it, his
	khopesh raised. Thebes prayed to him before a war. I prayed before the next hall, and my arm felt ten years
	younger: Might up by five, for two minutes. Then it was my own arm again. He does not mind being asked twice.
	His table takes my spares too, and he works the magic of amulets and rings as Isis does.

[note.shrine-of-ptah]
title: The Shrine of Ptah
text: Ptah of Memphis in his skullcap, wrapped like a mummy, holding his sceptre with the djed pillar on it. He
	made the world by speaking it; he made my skin harder by my asking. Armor up by five for two minutes. The
	craftsmen's god keeps his work short and good.
	He joins two rings into a finer one, and takes my spares for something better than gold.

[note.shrine-of-amun]
title: The Shrine of Amun
text: Amun the hidden one, blue-skinned on his throne, the tall plumes on his head, rams on the wall behind him.
	I asked him for breath and got it: every wound closed, and for two minutes more life in me than I have. When
	it ends the extra goes, but the healing stays. The king of the gods is generous with those who kneel.
	He takes my spares and works amulets and rings for me, as every shrine's god does.

[note.shrine-of-serket]
title: The Shrine of Serket
text: Serket with her scorpion on her head, its tail curled over her brow. Her priests treated stings and snakebites;
	she still does. The poison in me went at once, and for two minutes nothing with a sting could make it stay.
	Her children bite me as before, they just don't matter.
	Her table takes my spares, and she works amulets and rings as the other gods do.

# Plan sky-iron-rings: written with the first sky-iron ring, from two gold rings at a shrine.
[note.sky-iron]
title: Sky iron
text: Two gold rings went onto the table, one came back, and it is not gold. Dark grey, cold, a fine lattice of
	lines etched across it like frost. Bia-n-pet, the old texts call it, the iron of heaven: it falls from Nut's
	body as shooting stars, rarer than any gold, and the gods' own bones are said to be of it. The boy king took a
	dagger of it into his tomb. The gem in it does more than gold ever let it.

# The prologue (plan journal-intro): how the archaeologist came here, a title and up to three paragraphs (p1..p3)
# per page. The statue is Anubis (the boss of the last level), the ankh the one won there; the club is the
# starting weapon (items.cpp).
[diary.arrival]
title: Saqqara, the third week
p1: The dig gave us nothing for nineteen days. On the twentieth, under the sand of the east trench, a statue no
	catalogue knows: a jackal-headed man, life-sized, an ankh in his hand, and no name on the base. The crew would not
	go near it. I am a scientist. I touched the ankh.
p2: Light. Then cold stone and darkness. No sand, no sky, no crew. One torch burns on the wall, as if someone
	expected me.

[diary.descent]
title: Later
p1: At my feet lay an old acacia club. Not a trowel, but it will do: something scratches in the dark down the hall.
	The corridor slopes down. There is no way back that I can see, so down it is.
p2: The first signs I can read speak of one thing only: the ankh, life without end. Someone built this maze to keep
	it. Or to keep someone in. If there is a way out, it is at the bottom, with the ankh.
p3: I will keep notes. Someone should know what happened here.

# The epilogue (plan win-ending-flow), written when the ankh is taken. The 12th hour of the Amduat: Ra's boat is
# drawn through the serpent "Life of the Gods" (ankh-netjeru), tail to mouth, and comes out as Khepri at dawn.
[diary.dawn]
title: The twelfth hour
p1: The ankh is warm in my hand, warmer than my hand. The last wall tells the twelfth hour: the sun goes into the
	great serpent, Life of the Gods, at its tail, and comes out of its mouth young again, a scarab pushing the new
	sun. Khepri. Whoever rides with him comes out with him.
p2: I went in after him. Stone, then sand, then air that moves. I came up out of the east trench as the sky went
	grey. The statue is gone. The tents still stand, and the sun came up over Saqqara as if nothing had happened.
p3: Life without end, the signs said. I do not know yet what that means. Someone should know what happened down
	there. Now someone does.

# The ring notes (plan ring-accessories): one per RingGem in order, [note.ring-<gem>], the numbers of the copper,
# silver, gold and sky-iron rings.
[note.ring-garnet]
title: Garnet ring
text: A ring, a garnet in it. Rings are worn one on each hand, two at once, and both count, even two of the same.
	This one heals me slowly, also with something at my throat: 1 health every 8, 5 or 3 seconds, copper, silver,
	gold; sky iron, a hundredth of all my health every 2 seconds. Not while poison runs.

[note.ring-lapis]
title: Lapis lazuli ring
text: Lapis lazuli, night blue with flecks of gold, brought from far over the mountains. My breath comes back
	faster with it: stamina returns 15, 25, 40 or 60 percent faster, copper to sky iron. Rings go one on each hand, two at
	once.

[note.ring-feldspar]
title: Green feldspar ring
text: Green feldspar, the colour of fresh papyrus. It does not keep the venom out, but it washes it out of me
	sooner: poison wears off 20, 35, 50 or 70 percent faster, copper to sky iron. It bites as hard while it lasts. Rings go
	one on each hand, two at once.

[note.ring-jasper]
title: Yellow jasper ring
text: A yellow jasper, a sliver of the sun. I walk and sprint 5, 8, 12 or 17 percent faster with it, copper to sky
	iron. A jump carries me no farther: the gaps stay as wide as they are. Rings go one on each hand, two at once.

[note.ring-crystal]
title: Rock crystal ring
text: Rock crystal, clear as air. My weapon reaches a little farther with it, 10, 15, 25 or 35 percent, copper to
	sky iron; a bow aims farther too. A knife does not become a spear. Rings go one on each hand, two at once.

[note.ring-amethyst]
title: Amethyst ring
text: An amethyst, the rarest of them. My blows go through what a hide or a shell holds back: a creature takes my
	hits as if a tenth, a fifth or half a step worse off, copper, silver, gold; two gold rings or one of sky iron, a
	whole step: tough becomes resists, normal becomes weak. What it is vulnerable to stays so. Only sky iron moves
	what it is immune to.
	Rings go one on each hand, two at once.
